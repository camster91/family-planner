# Family Planner Brand & Product Voice

## Brand idea
Family Planner should feel like a calm household operating system: warm enough for family life, precise enough to trust every day, and polished enough to deserve a permanent place on the wall or fridge.

## Personality
- Calm, clear, capable, kind.
- Never childish just because children can use it.
- Never corporate or productivity-theatre heavy.
- Confident but not chatty.
- Helpful without guilt, pressure, streak anxiety or dark patterns.

## Visual character
- Apple-like clarity, hierarchy, spacing, restraint and tactile polish without literal imitation.
- Android-appropriate navigation/lifecycle/permission behaviour.
- Original illustration and icon personality layered onto a quiet interface.
- Soft depth, strong type hierarchy and purposeful colour.
- Shared tablet should read from several feet away; phone should remain efficient one-handed.
- Night mode should feel deliberately low-luminance, not simply inverted.

## Custom graphics
Create an original, reusable graphic language for:
- app/adaptive/monochrome icons;
- onboarding;
- empty states;
- meals/ingredients/categories;
- weather/status moments;
- sync/offline/recovery;
- achievements and completion moments.

Avoid stock-looking clip art, random illustration styles, holiday-specific decoration and copied Dribbble assets.

## Copy principles
- Lead with the user’s task: “Add groceries”, “Dinner tonight”, “Use soon”.
- Prefer plain language over system terminology.
- Errors explain what happened and the next recoverable action.
- Empty states teach what belongs here without pretending data exists.
- Shared-device privacy/elevation copy should be explicit and non-alarming.
- Avoid shame/guilt copy for chores, meals, spending, missed tasks or inactivity.
- Keep AI wording grounded: describe suggestions and evidence, not claims that the app “knows” the family.

## Microcopy examples
Good: `You’re offline. Changes will sync when the connection returns.`
Avoid: `Oops! Something went terribly wrong!`

Good: `3 foods are best used soon.`
Avoid: `Your food is about to expire!`

Good: `Add 4 missing ingredients to groceries`
Avoid: `AI detected what you need!`

## Motion/haptics
- Motion explains state change, hierarchy or cause/effect.
- Completion may use a brief satisfying response.
- Undo remains available after destructive-feeling quick actions.
- Respect reduced motion.
- Haptics are subtle and tied to meaningful touch outcomes.
- No continuous decorative animation on the always-on tablet.

## Accessibility
Brand expression never overrides contrast, target size, text scaling, focus visibility, semantics or reduced-motion support.

## Reference rule
Apple and Dribbble references are research inputs. We may borrow abstract principles and patterns, never screens, assets, unique compositions or proprietary visual systems.
